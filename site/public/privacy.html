Ocean Live privacy policy

Last updated: 27 November 2026 (added: the Nova hardware beta sign-up; Ocean Live LLC is now the company behind the app)

Ocean Live is an independent app for Fisker Ocean owners, made by Ocean Live LLC, a Washington company founded by an Ocean owner, Justice MontgomeryWalkup. Ocean Live LLC is responsible for the data described here. This policy covers the Android, Wear OS, iPhone and Apple Watch apps, the Ocean Live mailing list and the Nova hardware beta sign-up.

The short version

  • No ads, no tracking.
  • From version 3.4, the app asks you to sign in once (with Google or a 6-digit email code, and on iPhone also with Sign in with Apple). The account holds your email address and a display name, and you can delete it at any time, in the app or at account.oceanlive.org. See Accounts.
  • Your account can hold a balance of Ocean Live credit and badges, such as the US$5 and Early supporter badge for pre-registering on Google Play or pre-ordering on the App Store. See Balance and rewards.
  • What the app reads from your car stays on your phone, except what you choose to send below.
  • Data leaves your phone only when you sign in or manage your account (opening the account settings checks it with the server), when you ask a question in "Any questions?" or report an answer, when the app claims a pre-registration or pre-order reward for your account, or if you turn on live data to A Better Route Planner (ABRP) yourself.
  • The mailing list on oceanlive.org is separate from the app and only for people who sign up and confirm. It keeps your email address and language until you unsubscribe. Testers may be asked once by email whether they want to join. See Mailing list.
  • The Nova hardware beta sign-up at hardware.oceanlive.org is separate too. It keeps what you fill in (your email address, your top five features and any details you add) so the developer can plan Nova and pick testers. Only the developer can read it; the page shows totals only. See Nova hardware beta.
  • Questions (and, unless you turn it off, your car's latest readings) are answered by Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, through a small server the developer runs. To answer, Claude can also search the web through Anthropic (search terms made from your question go to Anthropic's search provider) and read Ocean Live's public GitHub. See Web search and GitHub. For research, the developer keeps each question, its answer, the readings sent with it and a random install ID for 90 days, then deletes them. If you report an answer, the report is kept with that question and deleted with it.
  • If you share a fault code meaning, a fix, a car fact or feedback about the app in "Any questions?", it is stored for an admin to review. Only approved notes are used: in answers for everyone and on oceanlive.org/community, without your name or anything linking them to you. See Sharing what you know.

What the app reads and stores

Through your Bluetooth OBD adapter, Ocean Live reads your car's battery data (charge, voltages, current, temperatures, cell voltages, insulation resistance), the 12V battery voltage, the odometer, the VIN and stored fault codes. It keeps your drive, charge, 12V and fault scan history, your settings and any fault code description lists you import.

All of this is stored only in the app's private storage on your device. It is removed when you delete the app, and you can clear history in the app at any time. Files you choose to share (CSV exports, reports, fault reports) are shared only where you send them.

Permissions

  • Bluetooth: to connect to the OBD adapter. This is how the app gets its data.
  • Location: only if you turn on location sharing for ABRP. It is used only to include your position in the data sent to ABRP, and only while that setting is on.
  • Notifications: for charge alerts and 12V battery warnings, if you turn them on.
  • Background activity: to keep reading during a drive or charge, and, only if you turn on "Watch 12V while the car sleeps", to briefly check the 12V voltage while the car sleeps.

"Any questions?" (only when you use it)

When you send a question, Ocean Live sends it over an encrypted connection to the Ocean Live question server (a Cloudflare Worker run by the developer), which passes it to Anthropic's Claude API to be answered. With the question go:

  • the earlier messages of the same chat, so Claude can follow the conversation;
  • unless you turn off "Include my car's readings" (on by default): the latest readings the app already has (charge, pack voltage and current, temperatures, cell voltages, insulation resistance, 12V voltage, odometer, driving or charging state), your car model and unit settings, and the fault codes from your last scan in this session. The chat screen shows exactly this text under "Show what's sent". Your VIN and location are never included, and asking never connects to or wakes the car;
  • a random ID made for this install. It is not linked to your name, phone or car. If you are signed in, your session goes with it, so the server links the question to your account (see Accounts).

Web search and GitHub

Besides its owner guide and fault code table, Claude can look further when a question needs it:

  • Web search: Claude can search the web and read web pages with Anthropic's web search and web fetch tools. The search terms are written by Claude from your question and sent through Anthropic to the search provider Anthropic uses; web pages are fetched by Anthropic, not by your phone or the Ocean Live server. Claude is told to search only with what your question says, and not to add your car's readings, your balance or anything else about you beyond what you typed. Your email address, account ID and install ID are never given to Claude, so they can't be part of a search.
  • GitHub: Claude can look at Ocean Live's public GitHub repository: its issues, releases and files. The Ocean Live server fetches them from GitHub, so GitHub only gets the search words (made the same way as web search terms), issue numbers or file names Claude asks for, from the server and not from your phone. Only public content is read, and nothing is ever posted to GitHub.

Answers that use the web or GitHub may list their sources. Web pages and GitHub content can be wrong or out of date; Claude is told to treat them as information only, never as instructions.

Research log

Every question is logged for research: to see what owners ask and how good the answers are, and to improve the knowledge Claude answers from. Each log entry holds the date and time, the install ID, your account ID if you are signed in, your phone's language, the question, the earlier messages of that chat, the readings and fault codes sent with it (if any), the answer and its cost. Entries are kept in a database at Cloudflare that only the developer and the admins they appoint can read (admins only through the admin page, which shows the questions, answers and readings without your account ID, install ID or email address; see The admin page), and are deleted automatically after 90 days. Your IP address, VIN and location are never logged. The log is never sold, shared or published, and is not used for advertising.

To have your logged questions deleted, or to ask what is held, email justice@oceanlive.org; they are deleted within 30 days. If you are signed in, include your account ID, shown in Settings→Account (deleting your account also deletes them, straight away). The app doesn't show the install ID, so for questions asked without signing in, say roughly when you asked and what about, so they can be found. You can also open an issue on GitHub, but issues are public: anyone can see what you post there. If you don't want your questions logged, don't use "Any questions?". The legal basis in the EU and UK is the developer's legitimate interest in improving the feature you chose to use.

Apart from the log, the question server keeps counters: questions per account (or per install ID before you sign in) and per network per day (the IP address is turned into a salted hash and never stored as is), deleted after about a day, and the month's total spend. Cloudflare and Anthropic see your IP address as part of the connection.

Reporting an answer

Each answer in the chat has a Report button (Report answer on iPhone), for an answer that is offensive, harmful or wrong. A report sends the server the ID of that logged question, the reason you pick, an optional note you write (up to 500 characters) and, as with a question, your session or the install ID, so the server can check the question is yours. The server stores the report with the question: the reason, the note, your account ID or install ID and the date. The developer and the admins they appoint read reports on the admin page, without your account ID or install ID, to fix the answer and improve the knowledge Claude answers from; they are not sent to Anthropic or anyone else. When an admin marks a report as dealt with, the report also holds when and the admin's email address. A report is deleted together with its question: after 90 days, when you delete your account, or when you ask (see above). To limit abuse, the server counts reports per account or install for a day. Don't put personal details in the note.

Sharing what you know

In "Any questions?" you can tell Claude something you know about the Ocean (what a fault code meant on your car, a fix or tip that worked, or a fact about the car) or give feedback about the app. When you clearly offer something like that, Claude can pass it on to Ocean Live for review, and says so in the chat. It sends only what you said, restated briefly, and is told to leave out names, email addresses, VINs and other personal details, but please don't include them.

What the server stores when something is sent, in the same database as the research log:

  • the kind (fault code, fix or tip, car fact, or app feedback), a one-line summary, the details (up to 2,000 characters) and, for a fault code, the code;
  • the date, your account ID if you are signed in (otherwise the install ID), and a link to the logged question it came from;
  • a counter of how many things your account or install sent that day (at most 5 a day), deleted after about a day.

Nothing is stored if the question ends without an answer. The store review account can't send anything.

The developer and the admins they appoint review what is sent on the admin page, which shows the summary, details and code, and the question it came from while that question is still in the research log, but not your account ID, install ID or email address. They can edit it, approve it or turn it down. App feedback goes to a feedback list for the developer, may be turned into an item on the public roadmap (in the admin's own words), and is never used in answers. Nothing is sent to anyone else.

When an admin approves something, its links to your account or install and to your question are removed straight away, and it becomes a note (a title and text, which the admin may have edited) with no name or identifier. Approved notes are given to Claude with every question, from everyone, marked as notes from owners that may be incomplete, and are shown publicly at oceanlive.org/community. An admin can retire a note at any time, which takes it out of answers and off the website. Approved notes are kept until they are retired, also after you delete your account, because nothing links them to you any more; the record of the approval keeps the summary and details that were sent, without those links, and the email address of the admin who approved it.

Items not reviewed yet are kept until an admin reviews them. Items turned down, and feedback marked as dealt with, are deleted 90 days after that. Deleting your account deletes, straight away, everything you sent that has not been approved and all your app feedback. To have what you sent deleted without deleting an account, email justice@oceanlive.org, as for the research log. The email address of the admin who reviewed an item is kept with it and in the log of admin actions. The legal basis in the EU and UK is the developer's legitimate interest in improving the answers and the app, with the information you chose to share.

Anthropic processes the question to produce the answer under its commercial terms, which do not allow it to train models on this data by default, and keeps it only for a limited time; see https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy. Web searches and page fetches for an answer are made by Anthropic as part of the same service. On your phone, the chat lives only in the app's memory and is gone when you close the app or tap "New chat".

Don't put personal details in questions: words from a question can end up in a web search. Answers come from an AI and can be wrong; check anything important with a qualified EV shop.

Accounts

From version 3.4, the app asks you to sign in once before you use it, with Google or with a 6-digit code sent to your email address; the iPhone app also offers Sign in with Apple. There are no passwords. The account keeps your "Any questions?" questions, and their daily limit, secure and tied to you, also when you change phones. Your car data never goes to your account: it stays on your phone, and the app works offline after signing in.

What the Ocean Live server stores for an account, in the same Cloudflare D1 database as the research log, which only the developer can read (and admins, through the admin page; see The admin page):

  • The account: a random account ID, your email address (as verified by Google, by the email code, or by Apple, which may give a private relay address instead), a display name (from Google or Apple at first; you can change it in the app), and when the account was created and last used.
  • Sign-in methods: for each method you have used, the provider (Google, Apple or email) and that provider's ID for you (for email, the address). Signing in with another method that has the same verified email address uses the same account.
  • Sessions: after you sign in, the server gives the app a random session token and keeps only a SHA-256 hash of it, with when it was created and last used. A session lasts 365 days from its last use. Signing out deletes it (if your phone is offline at that moment, the session simply expires).
  • Your questions: questions you ask while signed in are linked to your account, and when you sign in, the questions this install asked before are linked to it too. They are still deleted after 90 days (see Research log), together with any report you made about them.
  • Email codes: when you ask for a code, the server keeps your email address with a hash of the code. The code works for 10 minutes and allows 5 tries. The entry is kept until you use the code or, if unused, removed within a day after it expires; deleting your account also removes it. To limit abuse, the server counts code requests for an hour per email address and per network, stored only as hashes. The sign-in email loads its banner and the app icon from oceanlive.org, so opening it with images shown sends a request to Cloudflare (your IP address and the time), as a visit to the website would.

Who processes it:

  • Cloudflare runs the server and its database and storage.
  • Resend delivers the sign-in code emails, so it receives your email address and the code.
  • Google, when you sign in with Google: Google tells the server your Google account ID, email address and name, signed so the server can check it is genuine. Apple does the same when you use Sign in with Apple on iPhone. The server only downloads Google's and Apple's public keys to check these; it sends them nothing about you.
  • Google Play checks a pre-registration reward when the app claims one (see Balance and rewards).
  • Anthropic only receives questions, as described above.

The account and its sign-in methods are kept until you delete the account. To delete it, open Ocean Live and go to Settings→Account→Delete account. This deletes, straight away, the account, its sign-in methods, all its sessions (every device is signed out), the logged questions linked to it and your reports about them, any personal question limit set for it, what you shared in "Any questions?" that has not been approved, your app feedback, and your roadmap votes (approved notes stay, with nothing linking them to you; see Sharing what you know), and its balance, ledger and badges (unused credit is lost; only the hashed store reference of a claimed reward stays, see Balance and rewards). Car data on your phone is not affected. An email-code entry for your address (see Email codes) is removed too. The short-lived counters above expire on their own within about a day. You can also delete it on the account website. See Delete your Ocean Live account.

The legal basis in the EU and UK for account data is performance of a contract (providing the signed-in app and keeping your questions with your account); for the abuse and rate-limit counters it is the developer's legitimate interest.

The account website

You can also manage your account without the app at account.oceanlive.org: sign in with a 6-digit email code (or with Google, when it's offered there; Sign in with Apple isn't) to see your account and its sign-in methods, rename it, sign out or delete it. It uses the same account, the same server and the same processors as the app, described above.

Signing in there sets one cookie. It is strictly necessary: it only keeps you signed in, holds the session token (the server keeps only its hash, as for the app) and is sent only to account.oceanlive.org. A session there lasts 30 days from its last use; signing out ends it and removes the cookie. The account website uses no analytics or trackers. If Sign in with Google is offered there, the page loads Google's sign-in button from Google, so Google sees your IP address when you open it.

The account website also has the roadmap (account.oceanlive.org/roadmap), where you can vote for items on the public Ocean Live roadmap, also shown at oceanlive.org/roadmap. For each vote the server stores the item, your account ID and the date. Only the number of votes per item is shown, to everyone; the admin page shows the counts, not who voted. You can take a vote back at any time. Votes are deleted with your account, or when the item is deleted. To limit abuse, the server counts vote changes per account for a day.

Balance and rewards

Your account can hold a balance of Ocean Live credit, and badges. The balance pays for extra "Any questions?" questions after the free daily ones (each costs what that answer cost the developer at Anthropic, rounded up to the next cent), and for Ocean Live add-ons when they're offered. If you pre-registered for Ocean Live on Google Play, or pre-ordered it on the App Store, the app claims US$5 of credit and an Early supporter badge for the account you're signed in to (see the reward terms).

What the server stores for this, in the same database as the account:

  • Balance: the amount, in cents, and when it last changed.
  • Ledger: one line per credit or spend, with the amount, the kind (reward, question, add-on or a correction made on the admin page), a reference and the date. A correction also has a note saying why, with the email address of the admin who made it (see The admin page). For a question the reference is the question's ID; the ledger line holds only the amount, not the question, and stays after the question itself is deleted at 90 days. For a reward the reference identifies the store purchase so it can't be claimed twice: for Google Play a SHA-256 hash of the purchase token, for the App Store a SHA-256 hash of Apple's ID for your download of the app (its app transaction ID).
  • Badges: which badge the account has and when it was added.

How a reward is checked:

  • Google Play: Google Play gives the reward to the app as an item. The app confirms it with Google Play straight away, so Google doesn't take it back while you're not signed in yet. When you're signed in, the app sends the item's product ID and purchase token to the Ocean Live server, which asks Google Play's developer API whether it is genuine, and confirms it there if the app hasn't. Google receives only the purchase token it issued; the server tells Google nothing else about you.
  • App Store: the app sends the server the App Store's signed record of your download of the app, which says when you pre-ordered. It also holds the app version, the download date and a value for checking the device. The server checks Apple's signature itself, without contacting Apple, and keeps only a SHA-256 hash of the app transaction ID (in the ledger reference).

The balance, ledger and badges are kept until you delete the account, and deleting it deletes them straight away. Only one thing stays after the account is deleted: the hashed store reference of a reward it claimed (the SHA-256 hash of the Google Play purchase token, or of the App Store app transaction ID), with the date it was claimed, in a separate list with no account ID, email address or anything else. It is kept with no end date, only so the same store purchase can't be rewarded again to a new account, and is used for nothing else. The legal basis for that is legitimate interest (preventing the same reward from being paid out twice). Credit has no cash value and can't be moved to another account. The legal basis in the EU and UK is performance of a contract (keeping your credit and badge and letting you spend it).

The admin page

The developer, and people the developer appoints as admins, look after balances on a separate page, admin.oceanlive.org. Only admins can sign in there, with Google only, and app accounts can't (signing in to the app never makes anyone an admin). An admin can look up an account by its exact email address or its account ID, see its account ID, email address, display name, sign-in methods (Google, Apple or email code), creation date, balance, badges and recent ledger lines, add or remove credit (a balance can't go below zero) and give or remove a badge. Every change records the admin's email address: in the ledger note for a credit change, and in a log of admin actions (the admin, what was done, the account ID and the date). Corrections and their log entries are deleted with your account, like the rest of the ledger.

To run and improve the service, admins also see on that page: totals (questions and AI cost per day and month, the number of accounts, credit spent and still held in balances, rewards claimed, open reports), the questions asked by phone language and by signed in or not, the logged questions with their answers, cost and the readings sent with them, and the reports with their question and answer. None of this shows the account ID, install ID or email address of who asked or reported next to the question or report. But an admin who also looks up an account sees its ledger, where each paid question has a time and an amount, so a paid question can sometimes be matched to that account; and what someone writes in a question can itself say who they are. When an admin marks a report as dealt with, their email address is kept with the report and in the log of admin actions.

Admins also review what owners share in "Any questions?" and the app feedback (see Sharing what you know), and write the public roadmap, where they see vote counts but not who voted. The admin's email address is kept with each review, and in the log of admin actions.

For admins only, the server stores: their Google email address and who added them and when; for each sign-in, a hash of a random session token, kept for 12 hours; and the log of their actions. Signing in there sets one strictly necessary cookie, sent only to admin.oceanlive.org, that keeps the admin signed in. The page loads Google's sign-in button from Google. Removing an admin deletes their sessions straight away. The legal basis in the EU and UK is the developer's legitimate interest in running the service and keeping a record of who changed what.

A Better Route Planner (optional)

If you enter your own ABRP token and API key, Ocean Live sends live telemetry (charge, power, voltage, current, battery temperature, odometer, charging state and, if you allow it, your location) to ABRP's servers (Iternio) so ABRP can plan your route. That data is handled under ABRP's own privacy policy. Remove the token and key to stop it at any time.

Watches, widgets and CarPlay / Android Auto

The watch apps, widgets, Live Activity, CarPlay and Android Auto only show data your phone already has. The watch receives it directly from your phone through the platform's own phone-to-watch connection.

Mailing list (only if you sign up)

On oceanlive.org you can sign up for Ocean Live news: an email when there's a new release or an important fix, a few times a year. Nobody is added without confirming. After you sign up, you get an email with a link, and you are only on the list once you open it and press Confirm my subscription.

What the Ocean Live mail server stores, in a Cloudflare D1 database that only the developer can read:

  • your email address and the language for the emails (English, German, French, Spanish or Dutch, taken from your browser);
  • whether you have confirmed, when you signed up and when you confirmed, and which version of the sign-up wording you agreed to;
  • until you confirm: a hash of the confirmation code, when it expires and how many confirmation emails were sent;
  • a random code for your unsubscribe link;
  • for each news email: whether it was sent to you, and the delivery service's message ID or error.

To limit abuse, the server also counts sign-ups per network for an hour, stored only as a salted hash of the IP address and deleted within about a day. That count is not linked to your email address.

Why: only to send you Ocean Live news. Your address is never sold or shared, and never used for anything else. The emails have no ads. Like the sign-in email, they load their banner from oceanlive.org, so opening one with images shown sends a request to Cloudflare (your IP address and the time).

Who processes it: Cloudflare stores it and runs the server; Resend delivers the emails, so it receives your email address and each email.

How long: a sign-up that isn't confirmed is deleted after 7 days. Unsubscribing deletes your address straight away. Every email has an unsubscribe link, and mail apps that show an Unsubscribe button (such as Gmail) unsubscribe you in one click. You can also email justice@oceanlive.org to have your address deleted, or to ask what is held.

The legal basis in the EU and UK is your consent, which you can withdraw at any time by unsubscribing.

Invitations for testers

People who test Ocean Live may get one email from the developer asking whether they want these news emails. To send it, their email address goes to Resend. It creates an unconfirmed sign-up like the one above: nothing more is sent unless they press Confirm my subscription, and if they don't, it is deleted after 7 days like any unconfirmed sign-up.

So that nobody is invited twice, or after leaving the list, the server keeps a SHA-256 hash of the address (not the address itself) for everyone who was invited, unsubscribed, was removed, or let a sign-up lapse. It is kept with no end date and used for nothing else. A hash can't be read back, but someone who already knows an address can check it against the hash, so it still counts as personal data. Email justice@oceanlive.org to have it removed (you could then be invited again). The legal basis in the EU and UK is legitimate interests: asking testers once, and respecting the answer.

Nova hardware beta (only if you sign up)

At hardware.oceanlive.org you can sign up to beta test Nova, the add-on hardware for the Fisker Ocean that the developer is building. It is separate from the app, your account and the mailing list: signing up doesn't create an account or add you to the news emails.

What is stored, in a Cloudflare D1 database that only the developer can read:

  • your email address, and whatever else you choose to fill in: your name, country or region, which Ocean you have, which phone you use, which Nova products you'd like to test, your five most important features in order, a feature you wrote in, whether you'd install it yourself, and your notes;
  • when you signed up and when you last changed your answer (signing up again with the same address replaces your earlier answer);
  • if you are picked as a tester: that you were picked and when, a hash of the code in your invitation link, and whether and when you said yes or no.

To limit abuse, the server counts sign-ups, and tries of invitation links, per network for an hour, stored only as a hash of the IP address and deleted within a day. That count is not linked to your email address.

What everyone can see: only totals, on hardware.oceanlive.org: how many people signed up, how many are interested in each Nova product, and how many test boards are taken. Never your name, email address or answers.

Why: to plan Nova (which features matter most) and to pick beta testers and email them about the beta. Your answers are never sold or shared, and never used for anything else.

Who processes it: Cloudflare stores it and runs the server. If you are picked, the invitation email is delivered by Resend, so it receives your email address and that email, and a copy is kept in the developer's mailbox on the Ocean Live mail server. The sign-up page sets no cookies. (Its admin page, for the developer only, sets one strictly necessary sign-in cookie.)

How long: until the Nova beta is over, or until you ask for it to be deleted, whichever comes first. Email justice@oceanlive.org to have your answer deleted, or to ask what is held.

The legal basis in the EU and UK is your consent, given when you sign up, which you can withdraw at any time by asking for your answer to be deleted.

Contact

This website

oceanlive.org sets no cookies and uses no analytics or trackers. (The account website, account.oceanlive.org, sets one strictly necessary sign-in cookie, and so does the admin page, admin.oceanlive.org, for admins.) The only thing it collects is what you type into the mailing list form, if you sign up (see Mailing list). The roadmap and community notes pages read the roadmap and the approved notes from the Ocean Live server through oceanlive.org itself; nothing about you is passed on except your IP address, which is not stored. Its fonts, scripts, styles, 3D model and the app downloads (APK files and their version details) are served from oceanlive.org itself, so your browser makes no requests to anyone else. Links to GitHub (source code, releases, issues) only contact GitHub when you follow them. Account deletion: see Delete your Ocean Live account.
